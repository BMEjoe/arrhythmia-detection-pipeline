# Figure notes

One entry per figure: what is plotted, the data source, the sample sizes, the statistics, and the result files read. These are records for the author, not captions. Generated by `make_figures.py` (sample sizes are computed from the files on every run); do not edit by hand.

**Common conventions.** Every figure is saved as vector PDF and 300-dpi PNG in `figures/`. Detector colours are fixed across figures and validated colour-blind-safe (dataviz palette validator: all-pairs CVD ΔE >= 9.2, normal-vision ΔE >= 16.3 on a white surface): LLE alone blue `#2a78d6` (circle), noise titration orange `#eb6834` (square), K3 aqua `#1baf7a` (diamond), K1 violet `#4a3aa7` (triangle). Aqua is below 3:1 contrast on white, so every detector is also identified by its marker and a label. Ordinal series (Lyapunov exponent within a family; Phase 8 condition sets) use a grey ramp with distinct markers and line styles. The plotted numbers of each figure are in `tables/` (CSV and Markdown).

## F4. Exclusion curves (Phase 10 P1)

- **Plotted.** Upper bound on the prevalence π of windows containing an additive chaotic component (y, log scale, % of windows) against the chaos fraction f (x: fraction of beat-to-beat variance carried by the chaotic component), for K3 (top row) and K1 (bottom row), by chaos family (columns) and Lyapunov exponent λ (lines; light to dark = increasing λ within a family). Points are the seven design fractions (0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9). Dashed horizontal lines mark π = 5 % and 20 %. A value of 100 % means no bound (the detector never detected that component at that f).
- **Data source.** Phase 10 Question 1, confirmatory: one 512-interval base window per subject (83 subjects), chaos added as x' = x + s c with f = var(chaos) / (var(chaos) + var(NN)), beat times quantized at 1/128 s, real ectopy kept; real-window detection rates from the 988 confirmatory windows. Values are the preregistered analysis output.
- **Sample sizes.** 83 base windows per (family, parameter, f); 16 (family, parameter) sets x 7 f = 9,296 additive series per detector. Real windows: K3 8/988 detected, U = 0.0146; K1 16/988, U = 0.0245.
- **Statistics.** π_upper(f) = min(1, U / p_emp(f)), the preregistered model-free bound (PREREGISTRATION.md Section 4); U is itself a one-sided 95 % upper bound on the real detection rate (max of the subject-cluster bootstrap 95th percentile and the one-sided Clopper-Pearson bound), so the curves are 95 % upper bounds, not point estimates; p_emp is the empirical detection proportion among the 83 spiked base windows. No further interval is drawn.
- **Result files read.** `experiments/phase10_final/results/conf/analysis/analysis.json`

## S6. Phase 10: verification of the noise-titration implementation (logistic scan)

- **Plotted.** Top: Lyapunov exponent of the logistic map (per iteration) against r. Bottom: noise limit NL of the verified titration (percentage of the series SD) against r. NL > 0 should coincide with LE > 0 (Poon & Barahona 2001, Fig. 1).
- **Data source.** Phase 10 Q2b verification (`verify_titration.py`), r = 3.50-4.00 in steps of 0.005 plus the three V1-V3 points.
- **Sample sizes.** 101 values of r; 10 noise realisations per r.
- **Statistics.** NL = mean of the 10 bisection noise limits; LE from the map equation (2e5 iterations); predeclared criteria in METHODS.md 2.5 (78/78 chaotic r with LE > 0.02 have NL > 0; 18/18 periodic r with LE < -0.02 have NL = 0).
- **Result files read.** `experiments/phase10_final/results/verification/titration_verification.json`
