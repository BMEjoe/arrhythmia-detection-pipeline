## 5. Open items (would need new analysis or new data; not done)

1. **Conclusion 8 (E14).** The author decides whether to restrict its wording; a systematic test of flow chaos
   under different beat definitions (fixed-step sampling vs event intervals) would be new analysis.
2. **K3 at 1,024 intervals.** K3's real-data rate rose from 0.3 % to 2.8 % at 1,024 intervals; K3 was calibrated
   (false-positive rate) at 512 only. Resolving this needs a calibration or null evaluation at 1,024.
3. **The 7 non-degenerate K3 real-data detections.** Their attribution to nonstationarity, sleep/wake transitions,
   low variability or 1/128 s quantization is untested; it needs nulls containing those features.
4. **Like-for-like titration ORs.** The adjusted (independence GEE) and unadjusted (exchangeable GEE) burden ORs are
   not directly comparable (ERRATA.md O3); a same-structure comparison would be a new model fit.
5. **Partial development runs.** Phase 10 development spike-ins (96/528 tasks) and robustness (36/132) are
   incomplete; completing them is new computation.
6. **Full reruns on another machine.** `reproduce.sh slow` was assembled and dry-run, not executed (about 60 h and
   the data downloads); cross-machine agreement of the counts is untested beyond the documented ulp-level effects.
7. **Monte Carlo repeat runs.** Detector and titration decisions come from one run with fixed seeds (apart from
   the 499-surrogate subset); repeated runs with new seeds would quantify their Monte Carlo variability.
8. **Phase 10 Section 13 future work** (unchanged): the original Poon-lab titration routine; ECG-based ectopy
   review; a masked titration with adaptive linear memory; detection limits for flow-like and high-dimensional
   chaos; chaos inside a sinus-node model; window-level surrogate calibration of titration.
9. **Night/day.** No confirmatory record has a start time; a real circadian comparison needs data with clock times.
10. **Licence.** No software licence file exists; the author should choose one before archiving.
